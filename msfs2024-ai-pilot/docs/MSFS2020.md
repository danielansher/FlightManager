# MSFS 2020, and the default 787-10

## Short answer

**No, the built-in AI Pilot cannot be modded.** It is compiled into the
simulator's own executable, not shipped as a package in Community or Official
that an add-on can override. There is no file to patch. Nobody has fixed it and
nobody can, short of Asobo doing it.

**But two things will help, and the second is the real answer.**

1. Some of the flakiness is not Asobo's code at all — it is settings and
   controller bindings on your machine, and there are three specific ones that
   cause most of it. Five minutes, no software. That is section 2 below.
2. This project replaces the AI Pilot rather than repairing it. It drives the
   aeroplane from outside through SimConnect, which is identical in MSFS 2020,
   and it deliberately avoids every mechanism the default 787 is bad at. That is
   section 3.

## 1. What is actually going wrong

Two separate problems get blamed on one button.

**The AI Pilot itself is unreliable, and got worse.** Users report it beginning
its descent far from the destination and frequently crashing on landing, with
reliability degrading over successive sim updates
([forum](https://forums.flightsimulator.com/t/787-autopilot-problems-2/673687)).

**The default 787's own autopilot is separately unreliable, and this is the
bigger half.** The reported behaviour is remarkably specific:

- **LNAV does not capture, especially after takeoff.** The autopilot light
  engages briefly and then disengages
  ([forum](https://forums.flightsimulator.com/t/787-autopilot-problems-2/673687),
  [AVSIM](https://www.avsim.com/forums/topic/637795-anyone-having-problems-with-lnavhdg-on-787/)).
  LNAV and VNAV buttons sometimes will not activate at all
  ([AVSIM](https://www.avsim.com/forums/topic/611803-787-lnav-and-vnav-toggle-not-working/)).
- **It will not hold an engagement low down.** A widely repeated workaround is
  to not engage LNAV/VNAV until after takeoff, and some users report needing to
  be at 10,000 ft before it behaves
  ([forum](https://forums.flightsimulator.com/t/787-autopilot-problems-2/673687)).
- **The descent logic gets the pitch and speed priority backwards**, slowing the
  aeroplane to 220 knots at FL260 on the way down
  ([forum](https://forums.flightsimulator.com/t/787-autopilot-problems-2/673687)).
- **It does not track the route** even when it appears engaged
  ([forum](https://forums.flightsimulator.com/t/boeing-787-autopilot-not-tracking/424717)).

If the AI Pilot is issuing commands to an autopilot that behaves like that, then
"works about ten percent of the time" is roughly the expected outcome. The AI
Pilot is not really the broken part — it is sitting on top of the broken part.

## 2. Fix the settings first — free, and it may be most of your problem

These are the three causes that come up again and again, and none of them is a
bug in the sim.

### a. A twitchy controller axis is disconnecting the autopilot

This is the single most common cause of "it engages then stops flying". A
joystick, rudder or trim axis with a few percent of jitter on it reads as a
deliberate control input, and the autopilot disconnects exactly as it is
designed to. Nothing announces it. Users report fixing it with as little as a
**3% dead zone** on the offending axis
([support note](https://support.fenixsim.com/hc/en-us/articles/12466496060175-Uncommanded-Autopilot-Disconnects)).

> Options → Controls → your device → each axis → **raise Dead Zone to about 3–5%**,
> on elevator, aileron and especially rudder. If you have a controller you do
> not fly with plugged in — a gamepad, a wheel — unplug it and test.

### b. A control is bound twice, or the AI Pilot toggle is bound to a switch

Duplicate assignments, or an autopilot-disconnect bound to something being held,
produce the same symptom. Two devices bound to the same axis will also do it.

> Options → Controls → **Search "autopilot"** and **"AI"** across *all* devices,
> and remove anything you did not deliberately set. If you have autopilot on a
> single toggle, split it into separate on and off bindings
> ([forum](https://forums.flightsimulator.com/t/how-to-get-ai-piloting-to-turn-off-and-stay-off/521586)).

### c. The assistance settings are fighting the aeroplane

Several users report autopilot problems disappearing entirely once piloting
assistance is turned off
([forum](https://forums.flightsimulator.com/t/boeing-787-autopilot-not-tracking/424717)).
Note the awkward part: **AI Piloting appears in two places** — the Assistance
Options *and* the in-game toolbar — and they do not always agree, so it can be
"off" in one and on in the other
([forum](https://forums.flightsimulator.com/t/can-not-turn-ai-piloting-off/536929)).

> Options → Assistance → Piloting → set **AI Piloting**, **Assisted Takeoff**,
> **Assisted Landing** and **Auto-Rudder** to off. Then open the in-flight
> toolbar and confirm AI Control reads off there too.

Do these three, then try the stock AI Pilot again. If it goes from 10% to
reliable, you are done and you do not need anything else.

## 3. Using this instead

If the stock feature is still unreliable — and on the 787 it probably will be —
this project does the same job from outside the simulator.

```bat
windows\Check-My-Setup.bat      REM checks everything and says what is missing
windows\Try-It-Offline.bat      REM flies a whole flight with no sim involved
windows\Fly.bat                 REM asks where to, then flies there
```

It works on MSFS 2020 without changes: SimConnect is the same API in both
simulators, and `--msfs 2020` points it at your 2020 Little Navmap database if
you happen to have both installed.

### Why this does better on the 787 specifically

Not because it is cleverer, but because it **does not use the parts of the
aeroplane that are broken**:

| The 787's problem | What this does instead |
|---|---|
| LNAV does not capture or track the route | Never uses LNAV. Flies in heading mode and moves the heading bug itself, several times a second, from its own cross-track calculation. There is nothing to fail to capture. |
| VNAV descends wrongly, slowing to 220 kt at FL260 | Never uses VNAV. Computes its own descent path and commands vertical speed and target speed as two separate things, so they cannot fight. |
| The autopilot will not hold an engagement low down | Waits until 1,000 ft before engaging, which is real 787 procedure anyway, and flies runway heading until then. |
| The autopilot silently disconnects | Watches for it every cycle. Re-engages, restores the modes, and tells you. If it happens repeatedly it stops papering over it and tells you which of the three causes in section 2 to go and look at. |

That last row is the one worth having. Instead of an aeroplane that quietly
stops flying, you get a flight log that says:

```
[41:12] CRUISE    The aeroplane dropped the autopilot on its own -- re-engaging.
[47:38] CRUISE    The autopilot has now disconnected by itself 3 times. That is
                  almost always something outside the aeroplane. In order of
                  likelihood: a joystick, rudder or trim axis with a little
                  jitter on it, which reads as a control input (give it a small
                  dead zone); a control bound twice...
```

### Worth doing as well

**The Horizon Simulations 787** (formerly Heavy Division's B78XH) is the
community's standing answer to the default 787's autoflight, and is the most
commonly recommended fix
([AVSIM](https://www.avsim.com/forums/topic/637795-anyone-having-problems-with-lnavhdg-on-787/)).
You said you already have it. It works with this too — use `--aircraft b787-10`
either way. Its own LNAV is much better than stock, but this still drives the
heading bug rather than relying on it, so you get the same behaviour from both.

## 4. What to expect

It will fly you from A to B: takeoff, climb, cruise, a descent that starts where
it should, an approach, and a landing. What it will *not* do is fly a published
SID or STAR, avoid terrain, or talk to ATC. Do not point it across the Alps at a
low cruise level and walk away.
